\title{Query Performance Prediction Is Not Benefit Prediction: Observability for Selective Retrieval Interventions}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{\institution{Affiliation withheld for double-blind review}}

\begin{abstract}
Selective retrieval interventions, such as LLM query expansion (QE),
cross-encoder reranking, and pseudo-relevance feedback (PRF), are
often gated on a query performance predictor: intervene when retrieval
looks like it is failing. We show why failure is the wrong target and
measure by how much. Writing the decision as maximizing the expected
gain of an intervention given the telemetry a system can observe, we
decompose an oracle selector's gain exactly into gain on failing
queries, the most a perfect failure detector can recover, and gain on
healthy queries, which it never sees; with a per-query cost, a third
term charges the cost spent on failures the intervention does not
rescue. On TripClick and BEIR Natural Questions, across eight
intervention, corpus, and retriever settings, this failure-only
ceiling ranges from $8\%$ to $57\%$ of the oracle's gain, tracking how
often an intervention rescues a failing query ($1$--$46\%$) relative
to how often it helps a healthy one. Detection itself is ordinary QPP
(AUC $0.62$--$0.70$, no better than NQC under BM25), and it beats
predicting harm in seven of eight settings after Holm correction,
though the gap largely vanishes on queries the intervention changes.
Budget-matched routing confirms the limit: with pre-action telemetry,
failure-, NQC-, and learned benefit-based routing all stay close to
random routing and far below the oracle; benefit routing beats failure
routing only for reranking on NQ. Telemetry observed after the
intervention does better (on TripClick it cuts reranking's loss from
$-0.028$ to $-0.001$ nDCG@10), but only once its cost is paid.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Retrieval interventions help some queries and harm others. LLM query
expansion (QE) can rewrite a query using the evidence it
retrieves~\cite{csqe}, yet it harms a measurable fraction of
queries~\cite{weller-qe-fails,qe-fails-unfamiliar}; cross-encoder
reranking and pseudo-relevance feedback (PRF) are equally uneven. The
natural response is to intervene selectively, and the natural gate is
a query performance predictor (QPP): intervene when the current
ranking looks like it is failing~\cite{amati-selective,adaptive-rag}.
That the gate's target differs from the quantity a policy needs is
not new: selective-QE work predicts expansion benefit
directly~\cite{selective-qe}, and uplift modelling shows that
targeting high-risk cases is not targeting the cases a treatment
helps~\cite{ascarza-futility,causal-classification}. What is missing
is how much a failure gate gives up, why, and whether the telemetry a
deployed system can observe, its \emph{observability}, can do better.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\textbf{(C1)} A decision framing of selective intervention under
observable telemetry, with an exact decomposition of the
\emph{failure-only ceiling}, including a cost term
(Section~\ref{sec:framework}).
\textbf{(C2)} Its measurement for LLM QE (BM25 and dense retrieval),
reranking, and PRF on two corpora: the ceiling ranges from $8\%$ to
$57\%$ of the oracle's gain and is explained by rescue and
healthy-help rates.
\textbf{(C3)} Paired, same-query AUCs of detection and action utility
(benefit prediction) in all eight settings, Holm-corrected.
\textbf{(C4)} Budget-matched routing that separates pre-action routing
from post-action verification.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{QPP and selective QE.} Pre- and post-retrieval QPP
(Clarity~\cite{qpp-clarity}, WIG~\cite{wig}, NQC~\cite{nqc}) predicts
query difficulty. Cronen-Townsend et al.~\cite{selective-qe} decide
whether to expand by comparing the clarity of expanded and unexpanded
rankings; Amati et al.~\cite{amati-selective} gate expansion on
difficulty predictors; Lv and Zhai~\cite{lv-zhai-adaptive} predict a
per-query feedback weight; Collins-Thompson~\cite{collins-thompson-risk}
trades expansion reward against risk. These works evaluate the gated
system in aggregate; they neither bound what a failure gate can
recover nor compare gates at matched budgets.

\textbf{LLM QE and adaptive retrieval.} Query2doc~\cite{query2doc},
MuGI~\cite{mugi}, and CSQE~\cite{csqe} expand unconditionally;
generative expansions often hurt strong retrievers~\cite{weller-qe-fails}
and fail on unfamiliar or ambiguous queries~\cite{qe-fails-unfamiliar}.
Kotte~\cite{not-all-queries-need-rewriting} gates prompt-only rewriting
for dense retrievers (gate AUC $0.593$, no reliable gain);
Adaptive-RAG~\cite{adaptive-rag} routes on question complexity.
Neither separates a gate's detection accuracy from its benefit
prediction. In uplift terms~\cite{causal-classification}, our setting
is unusually clean: both outcomes are observed for every query
offline, so the uplift label is exact up to relevance-label noise.

\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

For a query $q$, let $R_0$ be the original ranking and $R_a$ the
ranking after intervention $a$, with metric $M$ (nDCG@10). The
intervention's effect is $\Delta_a(q)=M(R_a,q)-M(R_0,q)$ and its cost
$c_a$ ($\Delta_0=c_0=0$). With full information the best decision is
$a^*(q)=\arg\max_a\{\Delta_a(q)-\kappa c_a\}$, for a cost weight
$\kappa$. A deployed controller sees only telemetry $Z$, so the best
it can do is $\pi^*(Z)=\arg\max_a\{\mathbb{E}[\Delta_a\mid Z]-\kappa c_a\}$.
What matters is therefore what $Z$ reveals about $\Delta_a$, not about
$M(R_0)$. Two information sets differ in cost: \emph{pre-action}
telemetry $Z_0$ describes $R_0$ and is free; \emph{post-action}
telemetry $Z_1$ also describes $R_a$ and exists only after $c_a$ is
paid. A failure gate replaces $\mathbb{E}[\Delta_a\mid Z_0]$ with
$P(F\mid Z_0)$, where $F=\mathbf{1}[M(R_0,q)=0]$.

\textbf{The failure-only ceiling.} Fix one intervention and write the
cost per query as $\kappa$ (nDCG@10 units). The oracle selector earns
$G_{\text{orc}}(\kappa)=\mathbb{E}[(\Delta-\kappa)_+]$, and a
\emph{perfect} failure gate, which intervenes iff $F=1$, earns
$G_{F}(\kappa)=\mathbb{E}[(\Delta-\kappa)F]$. Because $M\ge 0$, a
failing query cannot be harmed ($\Delta\ge 0$ when $F=1$), and
\[
G_{\text{orc}}(\kappa)-G_{F}(\kappa)
=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[(\Delta-\kappa)_+(1-F)]}_{\text{gain on healthy queries}}
+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[(\kappa-\Delta)_+F]}_{\text{cost on unrescued failures}}.
\]
At $\kappa=0$ the second term vanishes and the gate's share of the
oracle is $\mathbb{E}[\Delta_+F]/\mathbb{E}[\Delta_+]$. The identity is
elementary; its use is that both terms are measurable offline for any
intervention and bound what any failure detector, however accurate,
can deliver.

\section{Setup}

\textbf{Data.} TripClick~\cite{tripclick}: 1.52M health documents, the
TAIL bucket's validation and test splits (1{,}175 queries each), with
click-derived relevance labels. BEIR NQ~\cite{beir,natural-questions}:
2.68M passages, human labels; 800 validation and a disjoint
2{,}052-query test set.

\textbf{Interventions.} \emph{LLM QE}: BM25 over SQLite FTS5 (Porter
stemming, title weight tuned on validation); a deterministic rule picks
one of four expansion operators or none ($1.5\%$/$1.9\%$ of queries
under BM25, $10.6\%$/$5.8\%$ dense), and \texttt{qwen2.5:3b} (4-bit,
greedy, $\le2$ calls) writes a corpus-grounded expansion. On TripClick
and under dense retrieval (\texttt{bge-small-en-v1.5}~\cite{bge-embedding})
the expanded ranking's top candidates are reranked by a MiniLM
cross-encoder~\cite{minilm-cross-encoder}; the original is not, so
those labels include the reranker's effect. TripClick's deployed QE
fuses original and expanded rankings (RRF); NQ's uses the expanded
ranking. \emph{Reranking}: the cross-encoder scores the BM25 top 100;
its top 50 lead, the BM25 order follows. \emph{PRF}: RM3-style~\cite{relevance-models}
weights from the top $d$ documents select $t$ terms whose BM25 ranking
is interpolated with the original (weight $\lambda$); $(d,t,\lambda)$
is tuned on validation over 108 settings ($(20,10,0.9)$ TripClick,
$(20,30,0.6)$ NQ). Reranking and PRF are applied to every BM25 query.

\textbf{Labels and models.} \emph{Failure}: $F=1$ ($45\%$ of TripClick
and $49\%$ of NQ test queries under BM25). \emph{Harmed}: $\Delta<0$,
ties not harmed. Detection is a logistic regression on 8 pre-action
features (score shape, term coverage, rare-term IDF, entity overlap,
coherence, perturbation stability; 6 under dense). Action utility is
fit on $\Delta$ from post-action features (for LLM QE: changes in
margin, coverage, coherence, rewrite drift, reranker agreement,
overlap; linear under BM25, a logistic harm model under dense; for
reranking and PRF: the 8 pre-action features plus overlap, top-1
change, rank-biased overlap, and the new ranking's score shape). For
pre-action routing, a \emph{benefit} model regresses $\Delta$ on the 8
pre-action features alone. Every model and threshold is fit on
validation and scored once on test; intervals are paired bootstraps
over queries ($10^4$ resamples).

\textbf{Evidence ledger.} No result here is independent confirmation:
every NQ query and the TripClick TAIL test set have been inspected.
\emph{Fixed before a test run}: the NQ BM25 and dense runs, and the
reranking and PRF runs, each under a written scope.
\emph{Pre-committed analyses of already-inspected traces}: the AUCs and
policies (Sections~\ref{sec:auc} and~\ref{sec:routing}), the
reranker-matched relabelling, and all of Section~\ref{sec:ceiling},
budget routing, and Holm correction, which recombine frozen traces
without new runs. \emph{Exploratory}: TripClick test was used during
development (an early verifier was calibrated and two apparatus
defects were found on it), and the changed-query columns of
Table~\ref{tab:auc} were added after seeing the main results.
Pre-commitments and scripts are in the anonymized repository:
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/agentic-query-expansion-8939/}.

A perfect failure detector would capture between $8\%$ and $57\%$ of
the oracle's gain (Table~\ref{tab:ceiling}), even though, by the
identity, it never makes a query worse. The share follows how often
an intervention rescues a failing query relative to how often it
helps a healthy one. Reranking rescues often ($17\%$ and $46\%$ of
failing queries, against $24\%$ and $55\%$ of healthy ones helped), so
half its headroom lies in failing queries. LLM QE rescues $6$--$15\%$
of failing queries and helps $15$--$25\%$ of healthy ones (a third to
two-fifths). PRF on TripClick almost never rescues ($0.8\%$, against
$12\%$), so nearly all its headroom is in healthy queries. The rest of
the oracle's gain sits in healthy queries that the same intervention
also harms often ($19$--$56\%$), which is why separating them needs a
benefit prediction rather than a failure one.

Costs widen the gap. At $\kappa=0.02$ nDCG@10 per intervention, the
second term (cost on unrescued failures) reaches $0.004$--$0.011$, and
the perfect failure gate's net gain turns negative for PRF on both
corpora and for dense TripClick QE ($-0.008$, $-0.001$, $-0.002$),
while the oracle keeps $0.011$, $0.025$, and $0.027$. A real detector
falls below these ceilings.

\textbf{Detection is ordinary QPP.} Detection AUC is $0.62$--$0.70$.
NQC over the top-100 BM25 scores~\cite{nqc} matches our detector on
TripClick ($0.647$ vs.\ $0.649$) and beats it on NQ ($0.721$ vs.\
$0.680$); WIG~\cite{wig} is weaker. The relevant-document count alone
predicts failure at only $0.570$ and $0.521$, and the detector stays
significant within count strata.

\textbf{Detection beats harm prediction, structurally.} Detection beats
harm AUC in seven of eight settings after Holm correction
(Table~\ref{tab:auc}), and the gaps persist when harm requires
$\Delta<-0.05$ or $-0.10$ in six of eight. They are partly structural:
failing queries cannot be harmed and are mostly left unchanged, so
the harm label is itself largely a failure indicator. Restricted to
queries the intervention changed, detection drops to $0.47$--$0.58$
and every BM25 gap vanishes; only dense NQ keeps one. Dropping failing
queries raises TripClick reranking's harm AUC to $0.659$, level with
detection. Predicting \emph{help} is easier where the intervention
helps often (NQ reranking: $0.679$, tied with detection). Two controls
hold: a harm model on pre- plus post-action features still trails
detection (TripClick BM25 $+0.040$ $[+0.003,+0.078]$, dense NQ
$+0.070$), and relabelling TripClick QE against the reranked original
leaves a gap of $+0.059$ $[+0.012,+0.107]$ (the dense runs stored no
reranked original). The action-utility score does rank queries by
gain for LLM QE and reranking (best-linear-predictor slopes~\cite{gates}
$+0.42$ to $+1.21$, CIs exclude zero) but not for PRF or dense NQ.

\textbf{Pre-action routing.} At matched budgets, every scorer that
decides before intervening stays close to random routing and far
below the oracle (Table~\ref{tab:routing}, Figure~\ref{fig:budget}). Failure routing beats
random by at most $3.2\times10^{-3}$ and falls below it for PRF on NQ;
NQC is no better than the learned detector (no difference survives
Holm). A pre-action benefit model beats failure routing only for
reranking on NQ ($+0.009$ $[+0.003,+0.015]$). This is the framework's
prediction: $Z_0$ describes $R_0$, and $R_0$ reveals little about
$\Delta_a$ beyond whether $M(R_0)=0$. For LLM QE, the deployed
failure-selective policy (median threshold, $49$--$51\%$ routed)
halves generator calls; on NQ it recovers most of unconditional QE's
loss ($+0.015$ $[+0.008,+0.022]$ over Static QE, residual $-0.007$
against BM25), and on TripClick no QE policy, including a closed-loop
agent, differs from BM25 (MDEs $0.007$--$0.011$).

\textbf{Post-action verification.} With $Z_1$, a controller can
compute the intervention and keep it only if the predicted gain is
positive; its compute is $100\%$. For reranking on TripClick, where
reranking every query loses $-0.028$ $[-0.042,-0.013]$, verification
keeps $15\%$ and loses only $-0.001$ $[-0.005,+0.003]$, while the
failure gate still loses $-0.010$. On NQ it keeps $94\%$ of reranked
results ($+0.223$ vs.\ $+0.225$) and $67\%$ of PRF results ($+0.0085$
vs.\ $+0.0097$). Post-action telemetry carries information about
$\Delta_a$ that pre-action telemetry lacks, at the price of paying for
every intervention. Reranking numbers use the current reranker input;
the stored TripClick arm used for the relabelling predates a fix to
that text ($0.285$ vs.\ $0.287$).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Failure prediction is the most accurate signal a controller has, and
it targets the wrong quantity: the gain a failure gate can reach is
capped by how often an intervention rescues failing queries, and that
cap differs nearly sevenfold across interventions. The
difficulty is not counterfactual missingness, since both outcomes are
observed offline, nor the generator alone, since reranking and PRF
show the same gaps; a likelier common factor is that pre-action
telemetry describes the original ranking, while $\Delta_a$ depends on
what the intervention retrieves.

\textbf{Limitations.} One small (3B) generator; reranking and PRF only
under BM25, with one cross-encoder and one PRF family. Two corpora;
under dense retrieval, retriever strength and domain are confounded,
and the dense QE labels include the reranker. TripClick's labels are
click-derived and sparse. The cost weight $\kappa$ is illustrative,
not measured. The TripClick QE benefit model is fit to the unfused
validation label, since no fused validation ranking exists. All
analyses reuse inspected test sets; every pre-committed quantity is
reported in full in the repository.

\section{Conclusion}

Detecting that retrieval has failed is ordinary QPP, and it bounds
what a selective controller can gain: a perfect failure detector
would capture $8$--$57\%$ of an oracle's gain across LLM QE,
reranking, and PRF, depending on how often each intervention rescues a
failing query. Pre-action telemetry, learned or not, routes little
better than chance at matched budgets; post-action telemetry
separates helped from harmed queries better, but only after the
intervention is paid for. Selective-intervention work should report
the failure-only ceiling and budget-matched routing alongside any
detector's AUC.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}

All data used (TripClick, BEIR Natural Questions) are existing research
benchmarks, used under their licenses; TripClick is used for
non-commercial research, and no model or per-query output fit on it is
released. TripClick's click logs are aggregate, de-identified
interactions, and we make no attempt to re-identify searchers. No
human-subjects data was collected. The generator is a small, locally
hosted model used only to rewrite queries within a bounded action
space. Our central finding cautions against deploying generative
interventions on the strength of a failure detector alone.
